Anoti Privacy Policy

Effective date: October 28, 2026

Anoti is a free app for Android and iOS that lets you know when new episodes of the anime you follow come out. It is made by Aleksei Vinogradov, an independent developer. This policy covers both versions of the app.

In short: Anoti has no accounts, no ads and no tracking. I don't receive or store any of your data. The only data that leaves your device goes directly to Shikimori, the anime database the app is built on, to load the catalog and check for new episodes.

Data I receive

None from the app. Anoti has no server of its own. It contains no analytics, crash-reporting, advertising or tracking code, and it never reads your advertising ID.

Apple and Google may show me crash reports and statistics such as install counts, but only if you allowed sharing with app developers in your phone's settings. They say this data doesn't identify you.

If you email me, I receive your email address and what you write. See Contact.

What stays on your device

  • Titles you follow. When you turn on a title's bell, the app saves the title on your device. It keeps the Shikimori ID, name, poster link, episode numbers, dates, score and status. It also keeps your progress: how many episodes you've watched and whether a new one is waiting for you.
  • Poster images. They are kept in the app's caches, so they don't download again.
  • Where you left off. The system keeps the open tab, your last search text and the scroll positions, so the app can reopen in the same place.

Your followed titles and the poster cache sit in the app's private storage, which other apps can't read. Anoti adds no encryption of its own. Your device's storage encryption covers them where the device provides it.

Backups. Your phone's backup can include your followed titles and your progress. On Android, if Google backup is on, Android copies them to your Google account. It also moves them to a new phone during a transfer, and restores them if you reinstall Anoti. On iPhone, they are part of your iCloud or computer backup, like other app data. These copies stay with Google or Apple under your account. They never reach me.

What the app sends to Shikimori

Anime information comes from Shikimori, a public anime database. Your device requests it from Shikimori directly, the way a browser opens a website. Depending on what you do, a request carries:

  • the list you're browsing (airing or announced), its sort order and the page number;
  • your search text, half a second after you stop typing, and again when you load more results or the app reopens your last search;
  • the Shikimori IDs of the titles you follow, to check them for new episodes. This happens in the background about once an hour. Your phone may run it later to save battery, and on iPhone, iOS decides when. It also happens when you pull down on the Favorites screen. If you follow nothing, the app skips this check;
  • a fixed filter that leaves out explicit titles.

Like any internet request, each one reaches Shikimori with your IP address. The app identifies itself as "Anoti". It sends no account, device ID, advertising ID or other user ID, and it doesn't keep the cookies the server sets. Poster images download from the links Shikimori provides. When this policy was written, those links pointed to Shikimori's own servers.

Shikimori runs behind DDoS-Guard, an attack-protection service, so requests pass through it too. Shikimori is an independent service with its own privacy policy, written in Russian. What Shikimori keeps, and for how long, is up to Shikimori.

Notifications

When the check finds a new episode, the app creates the notification on your device. No server sends these notifications, and the app never signs up with Apple's or Google's push service. A notification shows the title's name, the episode number and the poster. You can turn notifications off at any time in your device settings.

Sharing

Anoti shares no user data with any third party. I don't sell or share data, and I don't have any. Shikimori isn't my partner or processor, and I receive nothing from it. Your device fetches its public catalog directly and sends only what each request needs, as listed above.

If a future version ever shares user data with a third party, this policy will name it first. That party will have to protect the data at least as well as this policy does.

Security

All of the app's traffic uses HTTPS. Both versions refuse unencrypted connections, and the app skips any poster link that isn't HTTPS.

Keeping and deleting your data

Everything stays on your device until you remove it. I hold nothing about you, so there is nothing to delete on my side.

  • One title: turn its bell off, in the list or on the Favorites screen. Its entry is deleted.
  • Android: open Anoti's App info, tap Storage (Storage & cache on some phones), then Clear storage. Uninstalling also removes everything from the phone. Your Google backup may still hold a copy, and Android restores it if you reinstall Anoti. Android keeps only the latest backup, so after you clear Anoti's storage, the next backup replaces that copy.
  • iPhone: deleting the app removes everything from the phone. Copies in earlier iCloud or computer backups stay until those backups are replaced or deleted.

For anything Shikimori keeps, contact Shikimori.

Children

Anoti is for a general audience and isn't aimed at children. It always hides explicit (Rx-rated) titles, but titles rated R (17+) and R+ (mild nudity) can appear. I collect no data from anyone, children included.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the new version will appear on this page with a new effective date. Anoti's source code is public at github.com/aykme/Anoti, so anyone can read how the app works.
